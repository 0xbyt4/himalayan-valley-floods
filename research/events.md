# Event chronology and fact sheet: Budhi Gandaki (11 Oct 2026) and Bhote Koshi / Trishuli (26 Aug 2026)

Compiled 17 October 2026 from web sources read on that day. All times are Nepal time (NPT, UTC+5:45) unless marked.
Every line carries a confidence grade and a source number `[n]` from the list at the end.

Grades:

- **SOLID**: primary source (agency report, instrument catalogue, official situation report) and no conflicting source found.
- **LIKELY**: primary but marked preliminary, or a named scientist's analysis, or two independent secondary sources agree.
- **UNCERTAIN**: single secondary source (news, blog), an approximate value, or a value derived here.
- **DISPUTED**: credible sources disagree; the range and the names are given.

Coordinate methods used in the tables:

- `USGS-sat`: USGS catalogue location, which USGS says it "obtained from satellite imagery".
- `CLaSH`: coordinate printed in the Center for Land Surface Hazards (CLaSH, University of Michigan, NSF) StoryMaps.
- `OSM`: OpenStreetMap Nominatim geocoder, queried 17 Oct 2026 (ODbL). Point is the OSM node for the named place, not a
  surveyed site of damage.
- `other`: named in the row.

Weekdays checked: 26 Aug 2026 was a Wednesday; 11 Oct 2026 was a Sunday.

## Part A. Budhi Gandaki flash flood, Gorkha district, 11 October 2026

### A1. Summary of the mechanism (as of 17 Oct)

| Statement | Grade | Src |
|---|---|---|
| A slope failure of snow, glacier ice and debris started high in the upper Ungdilung Khola (also spelled Ungilung, Uingulung, Wingilung), Chumnubri RM-6, between Chamar Himal and Syerang Himal. NDRRMA gives a snow avalanche at ~6,000 m and a main landslide with glacier ice at ~5,500 m, in weak rocks of the Tibetan Tethys Himalaya. | LIKELY (official, "based on technical report", preliminary) | [5] |
| The mixed flow ran south down the Ungdilung valley and rose up to ~300 m on the valley sides. | LIKELY | [5][17] |
| Debris built up at the narrow Ungdilung - Syar Khola confluence at ~2,000 m and blocked and released the Syar Khola several times. A similar blockage may have formed at the Syar Khola - Budhi Gandaki confluence. This produced several flood surges. | LIKELY | [5] |
| About one hour before the highest surge, flow in Syar Khola stopped; field observation indicates water ponded for ~1.5 km upstream. | LIKELY | [5] |
| NDRRMA states that debris from Ungdilung Khola contributed more to flood height than the water impounded in Syar Khola. | LIKELY | [5] |
| The flow was a viscous mudflow with an unusually high fraction of fine soil. | LIKELY | [5][16] |
| Cause labels differ between sources and dates: "landslide-dam breach" (ICIMOD 11 Oct [1]; DHM/FFD 11 Oct [7]); "landslide from Siringe Himal blocked the Uingulung, then the Budhigandaki" (NDRRMA SitRep 1-2 [3][4]); "rock avalanche and glacier collapse confirmed by satellite imagery" (ICIMOD 13 Oct [2]); "snow avalanche + landslide with glacier ice + repeated debris blockage" (NDRRMA SitRep 3 [5]); "dry landslide in Philim area" (Nepal Army reconnaissance, per news [21]). | DISPUTED | [1][2][3][4][5][7][21] |
| No significant rainfall in the catchment before the event. | LIKELY | [7][11][19] |
| Debris on valley walls >500 m above the valley floor suggests an air blast from the avalanche. | UNCERTAIN (one scientific group, imagery interpretation) | [9][10] |

## Part B. Bhote Koshi / Trishuli flood, Rasuwa district, 26 August 2026

### B1. Summary of the mechanism (as of 17 Oct)

| Statement | Grade | Src |
|---|---|---|
| A rock-and-ice mass failed on the north side of Langtang Lirung (7,227 m) / Tsangbu Ri, on Nepali territory, and fell into the Lende Khola (Lhende Khola), a transboundary tributary that joins the Bhote Koshi at the Rasuwagadhi - Gyirong border. | SOLID | [24][27][29][30] |
| The failure was mainly bedrock carrying part of a glacier; a bedrock failure "sheared off part of an overlying glacier". | LIKELY | [27][30][43] |
| USGS reclassified the seismic signal from an M4.4 earthquake to an M5.2 "glacial collapse and debris flow"; "no earthquake had occurred". | SOLID | [24] |
| The avalanche became a debris flow and then a sediment-laden flood; total runout about 100 km. | SOLID | [26][41] |
| Not a glacial lake outburst flood (GLOF); no lake at the origin. | SOLID (ICIMOD, USGS, RDNA) | [29][30][32] |
| The brief's "temporary dam held ~18-19 h, breach ~08:30" comes from an IRDR rapid analysis (28 Aug, Adhikari et al.). It places the failure at 13:00-14:00 on 25 Aug at ~5,000 m and reads the 08:37 signal as the dam breach. ADRC repeats it as fact. USGS, CLaSH, ICIMOD (13 Oct synthesis) and the RDNA place the failure itself at 08:37:10 on 26 Aug. CLaSH notes the flood reached Gyirong ~7 min after the seismic onset, "not enough time to have constructed a significant lake". | DISPUTED (majority view: no long-lived dam before the flood) | [31][29][24][27][30] |
| A dam-and-breach scenario was simulated and rejected on timing and flow-height grounds (independent satellite reconstruction). | UNCERTAIN (non-peer-reviewed) | [39] |
| Early labels: "GLOF" (Copernicus EMSR927 page; NDRRMA "glacial-related flood"); "earthquake" (Nepal Foreign Minister). | superseded | [33][29][46] |
| Post-event barrier lakes formed after the flood (see B4). | SOLID | [27][38][45] |
| No heavy rainfall on 25-26 Aug in Rasuwa. | SOLID | [43][31] |

## Part C. Open questions

Budhi Gandaki:

1. Was the source a rock avalanche with glacier ice (ICIMOD 13 Oct, NDRRMA SitRep 3) or a "dry landslide" (Army)? No technical report is public yet.
2. Failure time: 04:20 (NDRRMA SitRep 3), 04:22-04:24 (seismic) or 05:30 (SitRep 1-2). Did an earlier failure occur around 5 Oct?
3. Where exactly was the main blockage: Ungdilung - Syar confluence (~2,000 m), Syar - Budhi Gandaki confluence, or both? Height, length and pond volume are unpublished.
4. How do the three seismic pulses (04:22, ~04:47, ~06:22) map onto the surges at Salleri (04:30, 06:30) and Arughat (06:30, 07:50, 08:30)?
5. Missing count: 5 or 6. Displaced count (654?) needs the NDRRMA tables; SitRep #4 (15 Oct) not yet read.
6. Volume: only one seismic estimate (7-45 Mm3). No DEM-differencing estimate yet.
7. Peak discharge at Arughat is unknown because the gauge was lost at 08:40 while still rising.
8. Petley links the 341 MW Times Energy project to the 1,200 MW government Budhigandaki storage project (225 m dam). These look like separate projects; check before using.
9. No Copernicus EMS or International Charter activation found for this event.

Bhote Koshi / Trishuli:

1. Did a temporary dam form on 25 Aug and hold 18-19 h (IRDR), or did the 08:37 collapse itself make the flood (USGS, CLaSH, ICIMOD synthesis)? Pre-image is 25 Aug Planet; its acquisition hour decides whether it can test the IRDR claim.
2. Volume range ~130-490 Mm3 depending on method; ice fraction 8% to 35%.
3. Arrival times at Rasuwagadhi, Timure and Betrawati disagree by 15-70 min. This changes the speed estimates by a factor of 2-3.
4. Which post-event lake is which (China MWR 2 Mm3 lake, NDRRMA 0.11 km2 lake, NRSC 19 ha and 11 ha, UNOSAT 12 ha and 19 ha, CLaSH 770 m and 530 m lakes)? Their status after 30 Aug is unclear.
5. What did the second event (M4.2, 11:45:35) do? USGS says it made a temporary dam and lake; its role in later surges is unknown.
6. Casualty figures: NDRRMA, Police, USGS and ICIMOD differ (missing 4,720-6,145). Missing lists contain duplicates and unidentified bodies.
7. Raw DHM gauge time series (Syabrubesi, Betrawati, Galchhi, Kalikhola, Devghat) are not public in text form.
8. Role of 2015 earthquake damage, permafrost thaw and the hot week before failure: proposed, not shown.
9. GLIDE number differs: FF-2026-000162-NPL (ADRC, Sentinel Asia) vs FL-2026-000167-NPL (Copernicus page).

### E8. Open questions after these reports

Closed or narrowed:

1. Budhi Gandaki missing count: 6 (C-Budhi Gandaki 5).
2. Rasuwa second event (C-Rasuwa 5): it made the landslide-dam lake at the Chhochen confluence, which drained on 28 Aug.
3. Rasuwa lakes (C-Rasuwa 4): two lakes, the landslide-dam lake (0.11 km2, ~2,800 m) and the impact-depression lake below the source. Matching them to the NRSC and UNOSAT areas is still open.

Still open, and needed for the model:

1. Budhi Gandaki: blockage height and length, pond area and volume, peak discharge, arrival times below Arughat. SitRep #4 has none of these.
2. Rasuwa: peak discharge in the upper corridor (Rasuwagadhi to Betrawati). Only Galchhi (~17,000 m3/s, preliminary) and Devghat (~5,850 m3/s) exist.
3. Rasuwa: arrival time at the border (08:40-09:05) and the reading of the slow fall at the Rasuwagadhi gauge before the event.
4. Rasuwa: water volume released. Values differ by a factor of ~5 (19.96 to ~100 Mm3) depending on site and method.
5. Two reports not yet read could hold raw gauge data: FFD "Technical Report on the 2083 Bhotekoshi-Trishuli-Narayani Flood" (cited by [57] p.32, PDF 22), and WECS / CHWRR "Assessment report II: Rasuwa-Bhote Koshi flood event, 2026" (cited by [55] p.25, PDF 31).
